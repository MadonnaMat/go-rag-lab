# Ulmarin Biology

The Ulmarin evolved in the lightless deep trenches of Vasa Prime, an ice-crusted
ocean moon warmed by tidal flexing rather than sunlight, and their bodies still
carry the marks of that origin. An adult Ulmarin is roughly two meters along its
longest axis, with a soft, radially symmetric body built around a central nerve
ring instead of a single brain — a design left over from an ancestor that had no
particular "front." Six manipulator limbs extend from this ring, each tipped
with a cluster of pressure-sensitive cilia fine enough to read the faint
vibrations of another Ulmarin's limbs from several meters away, which is how
they communicate in total darkness.

Ulmarin skin is bioluminescent, but only faintly — a survival trait rather than
a display one, since a dim light in the deep trenches was less likely to attract
predators than a bright one. Over centuries of living in lit surface cities, most
lineages have lost much of this glow; it survives strongly today only in a few
conservative trench-dwelling communities who consider it a mark of ancestral
purity.

Ulmarin have no lungs. They exchange gas directly across a thin, permeable
membrane behind each limb cluster, which means an Ulmarin's health is
immediately visible in the color and evenness of that membrane — flushed and
even is healthy, mottled or pale signals illness or exhaustion. This
transparency is a major reason Ulmarin culture developed such an aversion to
concealment: it is very hard to hide how you're doing from a species that can
read exhaustion off your skin at a glance.
