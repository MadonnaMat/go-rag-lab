# Ulmarin Language

Ulmarin language began as touch, not sound. With six limbs each ending in
vibration-sensitive cilia, early Ulmarin communicated by pressing limbs
together and modulating pressure and rhythm — a channel that works in
absolute darkness and total silence, which describes most of Vasa Prime's
deep trenches. This tactile root is still visible in the language today:
grammatical mood is marked by rhythm rather than word choice, so the same
sentence "spoken" urgently versus casually can differ only in timing, a
distinction that is difficult for translation devices built for
sound-based species to render at all.

As Ulmarin moved into open water and later onto the ice-shell surface, a
second channel developed alongside touch: modulated bioluminescent flicker,
readable at a distance where limb contact wasn't possible. Because glow
faded from most surface lineages after the Long Argument, most modern
Ulmarin speak an evolved form that keeps the flicker-language's grammar
but expresses it through fast, rhythmic limb gestures instead of actual
light — meaning two Ulmarin conversing today may be executing the historical
"grammar of light" without either of them glowing at all.

Written Ulmarin is a comparatively recent invention, adapted from
vent-forge crystallization patterns, and reads as a sequence of raised
ridges meant to be read by touch rather than sight — even Ulmarin who have
lived their whole lives under surface light still typically read by running
a limb along the page.
