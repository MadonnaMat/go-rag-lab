# Ulmarin Culture

Because an Ulmarin's condition is written plainly on its skin, Ulmarin culture
places almost no value on stoicism and considerable value on noticing. The
central social unit is the *drift-circle*, a group of six to ten Ulmarin who
commit to literally watching over one another — checking membrane color,
noting a limb held stiffly, and acting on it before anyone has to ask for help.
Leaving a drift-circle is not shameful, but forming one and then neglecting it
is considered one of the worst things an Ulmarin can do to another.

Art and status both run through pattern rather than possession. Since
bioluminescence faded from most surface lineages, Ulmarin developed pigment
tattooing applied in slow-healing layers over a lifetime, and a stranger's
social standing can be read directly from the density and symmetry of their
pattern — a young Ulmarin's skin is nearly bare, while an elder's is dense with
decades of layered work commemorating drift-circles joined, crises survived,
and skills mastered. Because the tattooing process is slow and cannot be
rushed or faked convincingly, it functions as an unforgeable record, and
Ulmarin regard cultures that rely on spoken reputation alone as faintly naive.

Ulmarin cuisine centers on cultivated trench-vent fungi, prized less for
flavor than for the ritual of preparing it communally — a meal cooked alone is
considered barely a meal at all, something eaten only when ill or in mourning.
